The Otty window, compact and roomy

How the one Otty window looks and feels at every width, drawn from the plan in BRAINSTORM (“Quick view and the Otty window”) and UX_REVIEW A1 to A11. Then a review of every place Otty and the user meet today, and a catalogue of the micro-interactions that would make each one feel alive, trustworthy and calm.

Look only. No code, IPC or schema in this draft. Uses Otty’s tokens from styles.css Follows your light or dark setting
What changed in v0.2
  • Motion is slower and more buoyant: touch stays instant, everything Otty does arrives like water.
  • Record is now Logbook, and What Otty knows is now Notebook. Reasons are in “Naming”.
  • The Logbook drops the filter pills for a list of views beside a ruled, time-stamped ledger.
  • New: a sectioned Settings screen. Home grows toward the long-term vision, with a switch to show what ships at which stage.
Reading the plan

What the design has to do

The otter is the centre

The roomy window is the otter’s home, not an admin console. Home opens on Otty, large, with what it is doing in one line beneath it.

BRAINSTORM · rules so it stays Otty
Width decides layout, the button decides behaviour

One screen at a time when narrow, list beside content at medium, sidebar plus a details pane when wide. Expand / Shrink is the only way to change how the window behaves.

UX_REVIEW A1, A3
Safety fits in 380 px

Pause, Undo, confirm cards and “what left my Mac” live in the compact frame too: Pause is a card when wide, an icon on the rail at medium, and in the header when compact.

UX_REVIEW A10
Only the unusual grows

Rows are one line. Detail opens beside a list when wide, or as a page when narrow. A card only gets louder when something is off the usual path.

UX_REVIEW, the rule
Inviting, with a reason to come back

Plain words, a greeting, and things that change: your day, your goals, what Otty noticed. Home is worth opening even when nothing needs you.

BRAINSTORM · for everyone
Alive within half a percent of a core

Movement happens because something happened. Ambient motion is stepped and sleeps with the window. The otter carries state so text doesn’t have to.

Naming

A refresher

What the den is, and how it differs from Home

The den is not the Home screen. It’s a concept in BRAINSTORM (“Otty’s den: feeding, play and cosmetics”): a cosy pet home where using Otty looks after Otty. Home is where Otty looks after you.

The den, from BRAINSTORM

  • Feed: each question is a meal, and each kind of capture is its own food. Dropping a file on the otter is the literal version. A cracked shell is the “got it” moment.
  • Play: pet, tickle or toss a shell. All local, no model calls, no cost.
  • Earn: shells for the first use of each feature, for variety, and a small daily allowance. Never for volume or spend.
  • Keepsakes: a collection that teaches features. Locked ones hint at what to try (“Snip something on screen”).
  • Gentle stats: “12 new words this month”, favourite app, languages used. Counts only, never content, kept on the Mac.
  • Later: hats, scarves and den decorations, cosmetic only.

Its rules: Otty never gets hungry, sad or lost; no streaks, no “Otty missed you”; no rankings or invented numbers like “hours saved”; rewards never interrupt work; a Settings switch turns it off.

Home, long-term

From landing page to the place your day lives

Every block on Home earns its place by being a surface the autonomy plan already needs. Tick “Show what ships when” above the mock to see the stage each one waits for. What’s on screen in the first release is the hero, Needs you, and Today’s past and present.

The otter and one sentence now

The greeting says what matters in plain words: how many things need you and what’s next. Later it becomes the Look after me greeting with a focus list (Stage 6). Tap the otter to visit the den.

Needs you Stage 2

The inbox AUTONOMY idea 8 asks for: confirm cards that waited, chore results to review, late runs. Oldest first, three at most, then “and 2 more”. When empty, the card isn’t shown at all.

Today, as a river Stage 2 to 4

One vertical line for the day: done items are muted, “now” is the bright point, and what’s coming flows below: scheduled runs, Otty’s own calendar blocks, meeting prep (Stage 4) and possible nudges, dashed.

Goals later

“Goals, not tasks” from BRAINSTORM: owned for weeks with progress. Straw when behind, never red. This is where honest pushback lands, warm and rare: “Part 3 has slipped twice. Move it, or drop the deadline?”

Otty noticed Stage 5

One offer at a time, from habits, in the straw “suggestion” colour. Always a plain yes, a no, and one line on what it would read and how to stop it. A “No thanks” shrinks the attention budget.

Looking after, and trust Stage 2

Standing chores with their next time, and one sentence on what left the Mac today, linking to the Logbook. Trust stays one glance away, never a dashboard.

Motion system

Water, not springs, and slower

Your touch is instant: presses and typing answer at once, because lag there feels broken. Everything Otty does afterwards arrives like water: it surfaces, settles and drifts, with a long, soft landing. Hover a token to see its curve, or press a demo.

Tap · 120 ms

Only for the user’s own press. Never slower. --t-tap

Quick · 280 ms

Hover, toggles, tooltips, colour changes. --t-quick

Settle · 480 ms

Cards surfacing, rows folding, panes sliding in. --t-settle

Travel · 720 ms

Window resize, quick view to window, picture zoom. --t-travel

Drift · 1.4 s

One-off flourishes that carry meaning: ripples, the Noted pen line, a permission granted. --t-drift

Buoy curve

A float that rises just past and comes back, for “done” and keepsakes. cubic-bezier(.3,1.22,.5,1)

Noted: you’re learning Japanese

A pen line drawn slowly enough to be seen, once.

  • Touch is instant, replies are water. Press feedback stays at 120 ms. What follows (cards, panes, receipts) uses settle, travel or drift.
  • Exits take about two-thirds of the entrance and sink rather than shrink, using the ebb curve.
  • Slow never blocks. Every animation can be interrupted: typing, clicking or a new message cancels what’s moving and jumps to the end state.
  • “Live” motion runs only while its state is live and the window is visible, stepped, and stops with data-asleep. The otter’s idle float is now 6 s in 10 steps, which redraws less often than today’s 4 s in 8.
  • Animate transform, opacity and a light blur only. Heights go through grid-template-rows; window size through one animated setBounds in main.
  • Reduced motion swaps movement for a 200 ms fade and holds the otter still. Every state also has a word or an icon.
  • Safety moments are calm. No shakes, no red flashes: stillness, a clear word, and a deliberate press.
  • Re-measure with OTTY_METRICS=1 after changing what runs while idle.

Deliberately left out

What not to add

Filter pills and badge soupRows of rounded chips read as generic. Views are text rows with counts; state is a word or a colour, not a capsule.
Eyes that follow the cursorNeeds polling while idle, against NFR-3, and new art.
Shimmering skeletons that loopTwo stepped cycles, then hold.
Confetti, streaks and fireworksThe den’s rules forbid guilt and pressure.
Shaking fields on errorFeels like being told off. The otter tilts its head instead.
Character-by-character typingSlower than the model and costly. Chunks surface instead.
Counting up money or tokensUsage bars fill once; prices only with a dated table.
Slow pressesWater is for Otty’s replies. A button that lags behind a click feels broken.
